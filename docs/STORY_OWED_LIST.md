# Story, part 2: The Owed List

This adds two things to [Grandma's Rival](STORY.md) without throwing out anything that's built:

1. **You inherit Grandma's lawn business**, not just her shears.
2. **The free yards.** The feel of the "I mowed this overgrown yard for free" videos: a neighbor
   who can't do it any more, a yard gone wild, a huge before and after, and the homeowner's face
   at the end.

Everything in STORY.md stays: the four acts and their gates, Gloria, TurfKing, Chad, Sam, the
Messages app and the Best Yard contest. This layer runs alongside it and pays off in the same
finale.

It's a pitch to build from. Names, lines and numbers can all change.

## The pitch

Grandma June didn't just win Best Yard. She ran **Hollis Lawn Care** out of her shed from 1971
until her last mower died in 2009. Then she kept her own yard perfect with a pair of shears for
17 more years, on her knees, which is what finished her knees. This summer she hands it all to
you: the shed, the rusty pickup with her name fading off the doors, the shears and the ledger.

The front of the ledger is customers. The back page is headed **OWED**: neighbors June mowed for
free, every week, for decades. Nobody's been by since '09, and their yards have gone wild.

So you run two lists. The front of the book pays for mowers. The back of the book is why the
business exists. Every free yard is the biggest, most satisfying job of its act. Mrs. Rossi from
bridge club films the first one from her window and posts it on **Clippings**, and suddenly the
whole town is watching.

And someone with very neat handwriting keeps sending people June's way.

## Decisions

- **June stays alive.** Inheriting means June retiring and signing the business over on her porch,
  not a funeral. She's a built NPC with about 100 lines, porch talk and a contest, and a living
  grandma who texts "YOU MISSED A SPOT" makes a funnier, warmer game. It also keeps us clear of the
  "dead relative leaves you the farm" opening other games are known for.
- **What you inherit is what new players already get free:** the Shed, the `RustyPickup`, the
  `GardenShears` and the bridge club customers. The story only names them as June's. The economy
  doesn't change.
- **Free yards pay no money.** They pay followers, a one-of-a-kind thank-you decoration for your
  yard, and the story. Followers turn into paid work through Fan Requests (below), which is how
  the real free-lawn creators make a living too.
- **The finale doesn't change.** The contest scoring is built and stays. The thank-you decorations
  are decorations, so helping neighbors helps you win without a new scoring rule.
- **No real apps in the game.** The social app is our own, Clippings. Nothing names or links to a
  real platform: Roblox restricts that, and many players are under 13.

## The Owed List

Five free yards. Each is gated by an act and by gear the player owns by then, so it's always
finishable (the same rule as the board's first slot). Each one:

- shows up as a pinned card on the job board: cream paper, a red **OWED** stamp, and "On the
  house" where the pay goes. TurfKing never takes it;
- is the most overgrown, most satisfying job of its act;
- hides one **keepsake** among the junk, which is the homeowner's story;
- ends with the full Reveal (below) and a short scene;
- gives a thank-you decoration (never sold, like the Best Yard Trophy) and a big Clippings post;
- drops one clue about 1971.

### 1. Hank Abernathy

The yard that sets the hook, in the first session.

- **Who:** 79, a Vietnam vet with a new hip and a walker, and Biscuit, a basset hound who hasn't
  moved since 2019. The Abernathys were the very first name on the list, in 1971.
- **Unlocks:** right after Edna's job. After June's critique and her "$66??" text, she adds:
  *"Now. Back page of the book on the desk. Read it."* The ledger opens at OWED. Most names are
  crossed out; the top one isn't: *"H. Abernathy, 14 Birch. Every Saturday. Since '71."*
  - **June:** *"Hank's hip went in March. Nobody's been by. Shears'll do it. Go."*
- **The yard:** smaller than Edna's (about 3 minutes with the shears), but shaggy and full of
  weeds, with the front walk half buried under creeping grass. Pure snip-snip.
- **Keepsake:** Sergeant, a garden gnome, face down in the tall grass by the porch. He's a
  special piece of junk.
- **The scene:**
  - **Hank:** *"You're June's? Huh. You've got her squint."*
  - **Hank** *(holding Sergeant up)*: *"Sergeant! Thought the HOA finally got him."*
  - **Hank:** *"Your grandma and the Pemberton girl mowed my mother's lawn every Saturday I was
    overseas. Never took a dime."*
  - **Hank:** *"Gloria did the edges. Don't tell June I said she was better at 'em."*
- **Then:** Mrs. Rossi texts the bridge club: *"I filmed the grandchild at Hank's from my window
  and put it on the Clippings!! 4,112 views!! Is the grandchild free Thursday?"* That's the built
  "free Thursday?" beat, moved to after Hank's yard and given a reason. Clippings opens on the shop
  computer.
- **Why it's free and still pays:** Hank's clip alone passes 100 followers, so the next job board
  already has a Fan Request paying 20% more. The free yard pays for itself, and the player learns
  that in the first 15 minutes.

### 2. Walter and Grace Park

- **Who:** married 50 years this August. Walter's coming home from rehab in a wheelchair, and the
  party is in the backyard. Grace lost her wedding ring in that lawn in May. TurfKing quoted them
  $400.
- **Unlocks:** at the start of act 2.
  - **June:** *"Walter's home Saturday. Fiftieth anniversary in that yard. Reel mower. Cut it like
    a putting green."*
- **The yard:** a Quality job sized for the reel mower, the cleanest cut in the game, plus
  deadheading the roses. It's the one job where the slow, quiet mower is the best tool, and it's
  where players learn that.
- **Keepsake:** the ring. It's tiny, and only shows as a glint in the uncut grass. A reel mower
  jams on junk instead of chewing it, so it can't be lost, but you have to find it.
- **The scene:**
  - **Grace:** *"Fifty years, and I lose it in my own yard. Walter, LOOK."*
  - **Walter:** *"Told you it'd turn up."*
  - **Grace:** *"I judged the very first Best Yard, you know. '94. Your grandmother had a partner
    back then. Ask her about it sometime."*
- **Then:**
  - June's porch talk gets a new line if you ask: *"Ask me about something else."*
  - **@TurfKingOfficial** posts *"Real pros don't work for free 👑"*. Two minutes later a message
    arrives from the same account: *"ok the ring video made me cry at work. wait. this is the
    company account. ignore this"*. That's Sam, a full act before they quit.

### 4. Memorial Field

The big one. The clip that goes viral.

- **What:** Maple Grove's Little League field. The town quit mowing it after budget cuts, and the
  Mudcats haven't had a home game in two years.
- **Unlocks:** in act 3, at level 8 (the Overgrown Lot's level) or with a crew of 2+. The ledger:
  *"Memorial Field. Every other Sunday. Since '71."*
- **The yard:** knee-high grass over the whole infield and outfield, plus a fence line, bleachers
  and two dugouts to trim around (`MowTallGrass` and `TrimAroundObstacles`, like the Overgrown
  Lot). A dozen lost baseballs in the grass.
- **Keepsake:** trimming around the home dugout uncovers a stone bench with a brass plaque:
  **"Memorial Field · Mowed free by Hollis & Pemberton Lawn Co. since 1971."** Now the sawn-off
  sign makes sense.
- **The Reveal** is the full drone shot. Then the neighborhood walks out, and the Mudcats play a
  game at sunset.
- **Then:**
  - The clip passes a million views.
  - For the first time all game, June doesn't text back after a job. After the next one: *"Saw the
    bench. We'll talk after the contest."*
  - Meanwhile Chad films "TurfKing Gives Back 👑" and mows over a koi pond. **Mrs. Rossi:** *"Did
    you see Chad's video?? The koi are FINE. Chad is NOT."*

### 5. Gloria's backyard (the epilogue)

It plays after the first contest's Collect, win or lose. Losing doesn't block the ending, because
this was never about the trophy.

- **June** *(on the porch, quiet)*: *"One more on the list. I wrote it the night she quit."*
- The ledger's last line, in 1994 ink: *"G.P. Backyard. If she ever needs it. She won't ask."*
- **June:** *"TurfKing only does her front. Judges don't walk round back."*
- **The yard:** a jungle. Hedges over the fence, grass to your waist, and in the middle of it,
  rusting, the red 1971 push mower. Leaning on her shed: **PEMBERTON LAWN CO.**, the other half of
  the sign.
- **Sam:** *"want me to film this one?"* **June:** *"No. Not this one."* The Reveal plays with no
  views, no comments and no followers. Just the yard, and Gloria.
- **The scene:**
  - **Gloria:** *"June sent you."*
  - **Gloria:** *"She always did the mowing. I did the edges."*
  - **Gloria:** *"Tell her the edges on her front walk are a disgrace."*
- **The last scene, at your shed:** the two halves nailed back together over the door, **HOLLIS &
  PEMBERTON LAWN CO. · EST. 1971**, with a new third board under it bearing your name. There are two
  rockers on the porch now.
  - **Gloria:** *"Your grandkid missed a spot."*
  - **June:** *"I KNOW."*
- **Rewards:**
  - the whole sign (a decoration with big appeal);
  - Gloria on the porch next to June from then on (porch talk takes turns between them);
  - **Old Red**, the 1971 mower, as a free finish for your reel mower. Hank fixes it up: *"Took me
    a week. She runs."* It uses the premium finish system (`Premium.applyFinish`) but is never
    sold.

## Clippings

The town's video app. It sits on the shop computer next to Jobs, Orders, Business, Crew and
Messages, and pops up as toasts the way texts do. It opens after Hank's yard.

- **Every finished job makes a clip** from the Reveal. The views tick up while you watch, and
  followers come with them.
- **Followers come from how big the change was.** A first rough rule:
  `tier base × how overgrown it started × finish × 2 for a Clean Sweep × 1.5 with Sam's drone`.
  Mowing a tidy lawn barely moves the number; turning a jungle into stripes does. That pushes
  players toward the satisfying jobs and toward finishing at 100%.
- **Owed yards have set numbers**, so the story's lines stay true: Hank's clip gets 4,112 views,
  the ring video about 50,000, Rosa's about 300,000, and Memorial Field over a million. Gloria's is
  never posted.
- **Comments** come from a pool in a config file, picked by what happened: hype (*"the edging at
  0:14"*), junk finds (*"NOT THE GNOME"*), Owed yards (*"who's cutting onions in here"*), stripes,
  speed.
- **Two regulars:**
  - **june.hollis** comments on every clip, in capitals. *"YOU MISSED A SPOT BY THE MAILBOX."*
    She's your first follower.
  - **gp_1955** comments on every clip, always about the edges. *"Edges could be crisper."*
    Players can work out who that is. After the epilogue the name changes to
    **gloria.pemberton**: *"Edges could be crisper. Proud of you."*
  - The two of them argue under your clips.
- **Fan Requests** turn followers into money. From 100 followers, a board slot (never slot 1) can
  show a "Saw you on Clippings!" stamp. That customer pays 20% more and asks for something from a
  clip (*"Can you do the stripes from the ring video?"*). More followers bring more of them.
- **Milestones** (numbers to tune):
  - 100: Fan Requests;
  - 1,000: an "As seen on Clippings" decal on your truck doors;
  - 10,000: a Golden Clippers plaque for the shed wall;
  - 100,000: a "Mow It Forward" mural on your shop.
- **TurfKing's account** posts a glossy ad every act. Nobody watches them.

## The Reveal

The end of every job: the PowerWash moment, and the last ten seconds of every free-lawn video.
It takes about 8 seconds, is skippable, and the pay breakdown follows.

1. **The last bit done.** A soft ding, and a sparkle sweeps across the whole lawn.
2. **The camera pulls up.** Early on it's a phone shot: Mrs. Rossi's, from across the street, a
   little shaky. From act 3 it's Sam's drone, looking straight down.
3. **The timelapse.** The lawn plays back from how it started to how you left it, in about 3
   seconds, in the order you cut it. The server records when each tile is cut (one number per
   tile), and the client replays that on the lawn itself.
4. **The homeowner walks out** to the curb and reacts: an emote and a bubble from their lines.
5. **The clip card** slides in: views counting up, followers gained and the top comment.

## The feel the story leans on

Most of this is built. The story's job is to put it in front of the player and make it mean
something.

| Satisfying thing | Status | How the story uses it |

| Junk hidden in the grass | Built (`Debris.luau`) | Every keepsake is a named piece of junk |
| Edges, stripes, flower beds | Built | Hank's buried walk, the Parks' putting green |
| Knee-high grass | Built (the Overgrown Lot) | Memorial Field, Gloria's backyard |
| **June's Eye** | New | After her first "You missed a spot", June shows you how she sees: hold Q (or a button on phones) and every uncut tile and edge glows, and the tracker says how much junk is left. It's PowerWash's show-the-dirt button, in Grandma's voice. |
| Section dings | New | Each part of a lawn (the front strip, the side yard, the walk's edges) dings and shimmers when it reaches 100%, like a finished part in PowerWash. |
| Clean Sweep | New | 100% of everything: a stamp on the pay breakdown and double followers. |
| The Reveal | New | See above. |

## After the story: Free Yard Fridays

New names keep turning up on the back page. Some of them are in Gloria's handwriting.

- One Owed yard a day (the daily reason to log in), built from a pool: who (a retired teacher, a
  new dad with twins, a firefighter on a week of shifts, a widower learning to cook), why they
  can't, the kind of yard and a keepsake.
- Each one pays followers and a **thank-you note**, pinned to a corkboard in the shed. Filling the
  board is its own collection.
- Best Yard still comes back every summer (built), and Gloria still enters. She just gloats from
  June's porch now.
